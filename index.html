Overview

Consistency matters

Match the appointment

Use the title that reflects the official status of the faculty member, not simply the type of course they teach.

Be specific

Rank, department, and appointment type often matter more than a general title when drafting bios, announcements, or references.

Check official policy

Institutional rules may affect when a person is called “Professor,” “Associate Professor,” or a clinical or visiting title.

Titles

Common faculty title categories

Title Typical use Notes
Professor Full professor, senior faculty rank Often used for tenured or established faculty.
Associate Professor Mid-level rank Common for faculty with a strong record and promotion to tenure track rank.
Assistant Professor Early-career faculty Used for faculty at the beginning of the tenure-track path.
Lecturer Teaching-focused appointment Often used where primary responsibility is instruction rather than research.
Instructor Teaching appointment Used for educators with academic credentials and instructional responsibilities.
Adjunct Professor Part-time or non-regular faculty Typically designates faculty who are not full-time employees.
Visiting Professor Temporary appointment Used for faculty temporarily filling a role while on leave or visiting.
Clinical Professor Professional practice and applied instruction Common in professional schools, clinical environments, and applied fields.
Usage

Best practices for writing titles

Use these guidelines

  • Use the official title from the appointment or HR record.
  • Use “Professor” only when the rank and appointment support it.
  • Include department or school when clarity matters.
  • Use “Adjunct,” “Visiting,” or “Clinical” when the role is special or temporary.
  • Keep titles consistent in publications, bios, and website profiles.

Examples

  • Dr. Elena Ruiz, Associate Professor of Biology
  • Marcus Lee, Lecturer in Communication Studies
  • Prof. Aisha Gomez, Clinical Professor of Nursing
  • Daniel Cho, Visiting Assistant Professor of History
